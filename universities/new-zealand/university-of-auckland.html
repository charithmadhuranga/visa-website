About University of Auckland

The University of Auckland (UoA; Māori: Waipapa Taumata Rau) is a public research university in Auckland, New Zealand. It was established under the Auckland University College Act 1882 as Auckland University College, a constituent college of the University of New Zealand, and was formally opened in 1883. In its early years the college operated from a disused courthouse and jail. It became an autonomous university under the University of Auckland Act 1961 and is administered under that Act together with the Education and Training Act 2020. Its Māori name, Waipapa Taumata Rau, was gifted by Ngāti Whātua Ōrākei in 2021.

The university is the largest in New Zealand by enrolment. In 2025 it recorded 39,183 equivalent full-time students (EFTS), an increase of 2,580 EFTS (7.1 per cent) on 2024, and a total headcount of 49,901 students. Teaching and research are concentrated on three campuses in central Auckland: the City Campus, which is the historic core; the Grafton Campus, home of the Faculty of Medical and Health Sciences; and the Newmarket Campus, developed on the former Lion Breweries site that the university bought in 2013.

Following a consolidation that took effect on 1 January 2025, the university is organised into six faculties, alongside the Liggins Institute, the Auckland Bioengineering Institute and other centres. It is the highest-ranked New Zealand university in the QS, Times Higher Education and Academic Ranking of World Universities tables.

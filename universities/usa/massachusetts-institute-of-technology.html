Overview

About Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT)

In 1865, the founding of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) established a new kind of independent educational institution designed for an increasingly industrialized America. Since that time, MIT has built a strong tradition of addressing problems in the public interest, operating at the intersection of technology and humanity.

The MIT community is motivated by a collective purpose — to create a better world through education, research, and innovation. Its members are known for being fun and quirky, inventive and artistic, passionate about numbers, and welcoming to talented individuals from all backgrounds.

Founded to accelerate the nation’s industrial revolution, MIT embodies an American spirit. Through ingenuity and determination, its graduates have developed foundational technologies, launched new industries, and generated millions of American jobs. At the same time, MIT maintains a profoundly global character, drawing strength as a magnet for talent from around the world. Through teaching, research, and innovation, the MIT community continues to pursue its mission of serving both the nation and the global community.

Why Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT)

  • Education: MIT fosters a culture of experiential learning, encouraging students to learn by doing. Across more than 30 departments within five schools and one college, students blend analytical rigor with curiosity, imagination, and a drive to tackle society’s most challenging problems. From science and engineering to the arts, humanities, social sciences, and interdisciplinary studies, the Institute offers excellence in every field.
  • Faculty: MIT’s faculty view research and education as deeply interconnected activities. Each enhances the other. In addition to pioneering advancements in their disciplines, faculty members play key roles in shaping campus life, serving as advisors, mentors, heads of houses, coaches, and committee members.
  • Leadership: The Institute’s board of trustees, known as the Corporation, is composed of 78 distinguished leaders representing engineering, science, industry, education, and other fields.

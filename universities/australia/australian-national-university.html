About Australian National University (ANU)

The Australian National University (ANU) is a public research university and member of the Group of Eight, located in Canberra, the capital of Australia. Its main campus in Acton encompasses seven teaching and research colleges, in addition to several national academies and institutes.

Established in 1946, ANU is the only university to have been created by the Parliament of Australia. It traces its origins to Canberra University College, which was established in 1929 and was integrated into ANU in 1960. ANU enrolled 10,252 undergraduate and 7,128 postgraduate students and employed 4,517 staff as of 2023. The university's endowment stood at A$1.8 billion as of 2018.

ANU counts six Nobel laureates and 49 Rhodes scholars among its faculty and alumni. The university has educated the incumbent Governor-General of Australia, two former prime ministers, and more than a dozen current heads of government departments of Australia. The latest releases of ANU's scholarly publications are held through ANU Press online. The entry requirements of many programmes are selective with high ATAR scores requirements. ANU has consistently been ranked as one of the best universities in Australia.

In 2024, Vice-Chancellor Genevieve Bell announced "Renew ANU," a $250 million cost-cutting and restructuring program designed to offset severe budgetary deficits and reduce expenditures by 16.5 per cent. The fiscal retrenchment led to widespread staff redundancies, with estimates indicating a net loss of over 1,000 personnel between April 2024 and late 2025. Academic offerings were also heavily impacted, culminating in the closure or downscaling of academic bodies, including the ANU School of Music, the Humanities Research Centre, the Centre for European Studies, and the Australian National Dictionary Centre. Following a 14-month compliance review completed in 2026, the Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA) found that ANU had breached three national higher education standards relating to corporate governance oversight, accountability, risk management, and sustainable operations. The regulator subsequently placed four binding conditions on ANU's registration—making it the only public university in Australia subject to active registration conditions. These mandates forced the university to initiate a Governance and Council Strengthening Programme overseen by independent, TEQSA-approved experts, alongside enforcing mandatory orientation, training, and information-flow reviews for university leadership. In June 2026, interim Vice-Chancellor Rebekah Brown revealed that a series of leadership scandals and executive failures had cost the university an estimated $100 million in reputational damage.
